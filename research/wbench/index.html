Introducing WBench

How well does your world model understand
the world it creates?

Read the paper ↗View code ↗
On this pageOverviewBeyond a single frameFive dimensionsWhat we learnOpen research

A world model should do more than produce a convincing image. It should respond to actions, maintain a coherent environment, and preserve the rules of its world as an interaction unfolds.

WBench is a multi-turn evaluation framework for interactive video world models. It brings different forms of control into a shared benchmark, making it possible to study visual quality, responsiveness, consistency, and physical behavior together.

Beyond a single frame

A beautiful video is only part of the story. Interactive worlds must also respond when a user moves, changes an event, directs a subject, or switches perspective. Testing these abilities over multiple turns helps expose failures that a single clip may hide.

WBench covers four kinds of interaction across varied environments, subjects, visual styles, and first- and third-person views.

01

Navigation

Move through a world using text, camera poses, or discrete actions.

02

Subject action

Ask a subject to act, and examine whether the world responds as intended.

03

Event editing

Change what happens in a scene while preserving its surrounding context.

04

Perspective switching

Change the viewpoint and test whether the same world remains recognizable.

Five dimensions.
A more complete picture.

WBench combines specialist vision models with multimodal evaluators. Its 22 automatic metrics are validated against human judgments and organized into five complementary dimensions.

Video quality

How convincing is the generated visual experience?

Setting adherence

Does the scene reflect the specified world and subjects?

Interaction adherence

Does the model follow the requested actions and changes?

Consistency

Does the world stay coherent across time and viewpoints?

Physics compliance

Do events behave in a physically plausible way?

Progress is multidimensional

The initial study found no model that performed strongly across every dimension. The evaluation reveals different strengths and weaknesses, giving a more useful account of capability than visual quality alone.

A world worth interacting with must hold together over time.

The value of a shared benchmark is not just comparison. It is a clearer view of what to work on next: reliable control, persistent environments, and plausible responses to a user's actions.

Built for open research

The paper, evaluation code, and benchmark data are publicly available. Researchers can use them to reproduce the evaluation, examine individual cases, and assess new world models.
