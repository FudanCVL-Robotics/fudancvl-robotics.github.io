SceneEdit: Change The Detail, Preserve The World

A small request should not require an entirely different scene.

Visual editing is a balancing act. A model needs to make the requested change while respecting everything the request leaves untouched: identity, composition, lighting, and the relationships that give a scene its structure.

Make the intended change explicit

SceneEdit is a concept preview for studying controlled visual transformations. A request might change an object's appearance, alter an action, or introduce a new element without rewriting the scene around it.

This framing treats the target change and the preserved context as separate questions. An edit can look attractive while missing the user's intent, or satisfy the requested change while disturbing unrelated content.

Preservation is part of success

A useful evaluation would consider both what changed and what stayed consistent. The relationship between objects, the identity of a subject, and the overall composition can be as important as the edited region itself.

The concept also opens a question about repeated edits: whether a scene can remain coherent as several instructions accumulate. This connects precise editing to the broader problem of persistent visual worlds.

Looking at the whole edit
